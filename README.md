# Open Problem 3: A New Problem, Two Different Directions

Everyday Workplace Communication · Module 1 · Segment 3: Understanding Directions · **Stage 3.4** (COM-M1-OP-03), in the Swift AI Academy frame.

The whole activity is one plain HTML page (`index.html`), with its pictures and sounds inside the file. No framework or build step is needed. `Module1_Open_Problem_3_Stage3.4_Two_Different_Directions.html` is the same file, kept for downloading. It opens with a double-click.
The page is generated from `module1-segment1/` with `node scripts/plain-page.mjs scripts/op34-plain.template.html <out.html>`. Do not edit it by hand.

## Publish on GitHub + Vercel

1. In Vercel choose **Add New → Project** and import this repository.
2. Set **Framework Preset** to **Other**. Leave the build command and output directory empty.
3. Choose **Deploy**.

GitHub Pages also works: Settings → Pages → deploy from the `main` branch, root folder.

## How it works

1. **Your plan.** A short animated story opens the activity. It is set late morning at a fork in the road outside the campus. The tea-stall owner points left ("Take the road on the left."), and the auto-rickshaw driver points straight on ("No, no. Go straight on."). Ananya stands at the fork with an empty speech bubble, and the story ends: "Two different answers. What do you do?". The narrator reads each caption as it types in, and each person speaks as their speech bubble appears. It plays once, on its own where the browser allows sound. Browsers block sound until the visitor taps or presses a key, so otherwise the story runs silently, a "Tap to hear the story" button shows, and the first tap starts it again from the beginning with sound. The same button can stop the sound or play the story again. People who prefer reduced motion see the final picture. Then the script's question: "Two people give you slightly different directions to the same place. What do you do?" The learner writes what they would do, and why (5 to 50 words).
2. **Compare and reflect.** The learner sees their answer and a short feedback message. The message is built from an internal check: a sensible way to find the right road (several are accepted), and a reason. **The key words and the checks are never shown to learners**; staff can find them in the separate checking-metric document. Then the brief's ways that work: ask both again, ask a third person, follow one way and adjust, or check a map or the signs. They are labelled as ideas to compare, not a correction. Optional voice practice has a Practice badge and a consent tick box; it is recorded in the browser only and never checked. The learner can choose "Try another answer".
3. **Take it with you.** "Write the exact words you would say to check the way." (3 to 30 words). The feedback message comes from an internal check of five points: polite words, polite words first, a question, "could you" followed by what you need, and which way or place is meant (or asking them to say it again). Then come two ways to say it, to compare.

At the end: "Practice complete", **Download my notes** (a text file) and **Delete my saved work**.

- **Not graded:** a "Practice · not graded" badge and no score. Attempts are saved in the browser as `3.4.open-problem-log`. Nothing is sent anywhere; recordings are never saved or uploaded.
- The top bar shows "About 10 minutes" and never counts down. There is no Pause button and no time limit, as in the other Module 1 pages.
- No contractions are used anywhere on screen.
