# Open Problem 3: A New Problem, Two Different Directions

Everyday Workplace Communication · Module 1 · Segment 3: Understanding Directions · **Stage 3.4** (COM-M1-OP-03), in the Swift AI Academy frame.

The whole activity is one plain HTML page (`index.html`), with its pictures and sounds inside the file. No framework or build step is needed. `Module1_Open_Problem_3_Stage3.4_Two_Different_Directions.html` is the same file, kept for downloading. It opens with a double-click.
The page is generated from `module1-segment1/` with `node scripts/plain-page.mjs scripts/op34-plain.template.html <out.html>`. Do not edit it by hand.

## Publish on GitHub + Vercel

1. In Vercel choose **Add New → Project** and import this repository.
2. Set **Framework Preset** to **Other**. Leave the build command and output directory empty.
3. Choose **Deploy**.

GitHub Pages also works: Settings → Pages → deploy from the `main` branch, root folder.

## How it works

1. **Your plan.** The opening story uses the three painted scenes from the client's asset package, with soft cross-fades:
   - the tea-stall owner pointing left;
   - the auto-rickshaw driver pointing straight ahead;
   - Ananya at the fork, with both arrows and her empty speech bubble.

   The narrator reads each caption as it types in, and the owner ("Take the road on the left.") and the driver ("No, no. Go straight on.") speak their lines. It plays once, on its own where the browser allows sound. Browsers block sound until the visitor taps or presses a key, so otherwise the story runs silently and the first tap starts it again with sound. The sound button sits in the caption bar, so it never covers the painted arrows. People who prefer reduced motion see the last scene.

   Then the script's question: "Two people give you slightly different directions to the same place. What do you do?" The learner writes their plan and their reason (5 to 50 words). An optional "Need help getting started?" panel gives the package's three hints, which do not give a strategy.
2. **Compare and reflect.** The learner sees their answer and a short feedback message. The message is built from an internal check: a sensible way to find the right road (several are accepted) and a reason. **The key words and the checks are never shown to learners**; staff can find them in the separate checking-metric document. Then the brief's three strategies, with the package's explanations: ask both people again, follow one route and adjust, and ask a third person. They are labelled as possibilities, not corrections. Optional voice practice is available, and the learner can choose "Revise my plan".
3. **Check before you go.** The package's question: "What would you check before you set off?" (3 to 30 words). It is checked internally for one sensible thing to check: the place, the way, a sign or landmark, asking again, or how far it is. Then come ideas to compare.

At the end: "Practice complete", **Download my notes** (a text file) and **Delete my saved work**.

- **Only real answers are accepted.** Before any feedback, the page checks that the answer is a real answer to the question. It must be mostly real English words, with sensible content words; it must not repeat the same word or be just "I do not know"; it must be written as a sentence; and it must be on the question. Otherwise the learner stays on the question with a short message, and the attempt is saved in the record as not valid.
- **Not graded:** a "Practice · not graded" badge and no score. Attempts are saved in the browser as `3.4.open-problem-log`. Nothing is sent anywhere; recordings are never saved or uploaded.
- The top bar shows "About 10 minutes" and never counts down. There is no Pause button and no time limit, as in the other Module 1 pages.
- No contractions are used anywhere on screen.
